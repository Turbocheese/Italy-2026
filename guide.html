A guide for the six of us

How the itinerary
app works

Everything about our trip lives in one app — where we're going, when, what's booked and what still isn't. It knows what time it is in Italy, so it shows you the stop you're standing at. Nothing you tap can break it. Have a poke around.

01
Start here

Getting it onto your phone

1
Open the link I sent in the group chat. It asks whose phone it is — tap your own name. That's the only setup, and you can change it later under More.
2
Add it to your home screen. On an iPhone, tap the share button — the square with the arrow pointing up — and choose Add to Home Screen. On Android, tap the three dots and choose Install.
3
That's it. It opens like any other app. We all have eSIMs, so most of it runs live — but the whole plan still works with no signal, which matters inside the Vatican Museums and on the Palermo backstreets.
02
The main screen

One day at a time

The row of dates at the top

Tap any date to jump to that day, or slide the row sideways. Above it are the four cities — tap Rome and you land on our first day there. Each city has its own colour and its own pattern, and the whole app takes them on: Venice teal with ripples spreading off the lagoon, Florence terracotta with the dome's diamond coffering, Rome crimson with a Colosseum arcade, Palermo gold with majolica tile. If you can't remember what day it is, the header tells you what city you're in.

The big card, twice the size of the rest

On today, whatever is happening now grows to twice the size of everything around it — pale, lifted off the page, impossible to lose. Between stops it becomes the next thing, counting down. It stays that shape all day, so the biggest card on screen is always the one that matters.

The cards down the page

Every stop opens with a little symbol saying what kind of thing it is — a fork for a table, a dome for a church, a boat or a plane for moving about. Under the name: opening hours, why we put it there, what to do if it's shut, and how far the walk from the last stop was.

Guide me

The coloured button on any stop with an address. It finds where you're standing and opens walking directions straight there — no typing the name in. Maps next to it just shows you the place on a map.

Ticking things off

Swipe a card to the right and it's done — the card fills, the phone buzzes, and it settles with a line through it. Swipe again to undo. If swiping is fiddly, tap the circle on the left of the card instead.

Your ticks stay on your own phone and don't show up on anyone else's. Nobody can mess up your copy, and you can't mess up theirs.

03
Above the stops

What the app tells you before you start

Your morning brief
Waiting for you first thing: how long until the first move, the weather, what's up first, and the one warning for the day. If rain is likely, the wet-weather plan folds into it. It retires once the day is underway.
Golden hour
The fifty minutes before sunset when everything photographs well, worked out from that city's real sunset, with a countdown. Where we're already booked somewhere good for it — Piazzale Michelangelo, the Gianicolo — it says so. It doesn't appear on flying days.
Six little tiles
Temperature, chance of rain, humidity, sunset, how hard the day is, and a ring showing how much of it you've done. Real forecast when there is one; the usual September numbers until then.
Distances on foot
A dotted trace of the day drawn to true distances, with the dot you're at filled in. Underneath it says how far the day is end to end. Every stop also carries its own line — "480 m · 6 min walk · south-east of the Ponte Vecchio".
The striped bar & the day brief
Each stripe is a kind of stop, so one glance says whether the day is packed or gentle — tap a stripe to jump there. Tap the brief open for what to wear, what's shut that weekday, the rest stops for Grandma, and what to cut if we're running long.
04
While we're out

Two things that follow you

The bar above the buttons

On today, a slim bar sits just above the five buttons: the symbol for what you're at, whether it's now or next, the time, how long you've got, and how far through the day you are. It breathes gently so you can tell it's live. The round button on its right walks you to that stop. Ten minutes before anything with a fixed time, it buzzes once and a banner slides in — that only works while the app is open, so it's a nudge, not an alarm. Set a real alarm for the flights.

Evening mode

Seventy minutes before that city's real sunset, today's screen starts to darken — the page becomes a deep field of the city's colour, the header band inverts to pale like a moon over it, and the writing steps up a size for reading at dinner. A golden banner announces the switch once. It only ever happens on today; every other day stays light. The brightness button in the corner still overrides everything.

05
When we divide

Nobody has to do everything

Four times across the sixteen days the plan divides, and the app knows about all four. Because it knows whose phone it is, it shows you your half.

The four splits
The Duomo dome, where Grandma and Sarah take the Baptistery and the museum instead of 463 steps. The gondola, where Ryan and Ceci take the €2 traghetto across the Grand Canal. St Peter's at seven, after fourteen hours on your feet. And Ballarò, where sitting it out at a café on Via Maqueda is a perfectly good afternoon.
It offers, it doesn't decide
The stop says what you're down for, who's with you, and what the others are doing — then one line offers the alternative: 463 steps and no lift. Not climbing? Tap it and you swap sides. Tap again and you're back. The half you're not on fades but stays readable, so you can always see what you're missing.
Meeting back up
Every split names the time and the place you come back together, with a countdown on the day and a button that walks you there. Ten minutes before, the phone buzzes. While you're apart, the bar above the buttons tells you what the other half is up to.
06
Along the bottom

The five buttons

Convert
Singapore dollars to euros and back. Type an amount or tap a quick button. It fetches the live rate and remembers the last one, so it still works with no signal.
Spent
A running total of the trip, already counting what's prepaid. Add something with a name, an amount and who it was for — mark it Everyone and it splits six ways by itself. It totals by category, by city and by person, so nobody does sums at an airport.
Today
Lost in the app? This brings you back to today, wherever you'd wandered off to.
Italian
Promoted out of the drawer, because it's the one you need with a waiter standing there. A table for six, the bill, telling someone Ceci can't eat squid, finding a pharmacy. Tap the play button and the phone says it out loud, in Italian, slowly — hold it up if your accent fails you.
More
Everything else lives here — the next section.

Search moved up to the header — the magnifying glass at the top right. Type anything: a restaurant, a shop, "gelato", "leather", a booking reference. It searches all sixteen days, including the sixty-odd shops tucked inside the browsing stops.

07
Behind More

The useful drawers

Long list, but you only ever need two or three of them. These are the ones worth knowing.

Bookings & numbersEvery flight, train, seat number, booking reference and apartment address, plus emergency numbers. Every flight and train is a boarding pass — airports either side, times, and the reference in big type across a torn stub, so you can hold the phone up at a counter. If you only learn one screen, learn this one.
Live statusThe six legs that hold the trip together — both Turkish flights, the two Italo trains, Palermo out and back — each with a live-status link and the operator's own board. Underneath, the departure boards for every station and airport we touch. Check before you go underground.
Where's everyoneFor when we split up and meet again. Whoever's holding the group taps each name off as they turn up, and it says who we're still waiting on. One tap copies that line into the family chat. It's a roll call on one phone, not a tracker.
The day on the groundThe whole day on a real map, stops numbered in order, with a button that hands the lot to Google Maps as one route. The version that works with no signal is the plotted one on the main screen.
Who carries whatEach of us, our references and our baggage. The one to remember: the big suitcases go into left luggage at Fiumicino before the Palermo flight, so we fly to Sicily and back with cabin bags only — Sarah's is the single checked bag. Buy things in Rome, not Palermo.
If you're running earlyThe good things we cut to make the plan fit, kept per city with hours and map links. For when a day finishes ahead of itself.
Arrival modeA big, simple screen with just where we're going next and nothing else. Use it while walking, or hand it to whoever is navigating.
Packing, decisions & notesA tickable packing list, the handful of things still unbooked sorted by when they're needed, and the city notes explaining what moved and why.
For funTrails — every gelato, tiramisu, church and wine window as a collection to tick off. Passport — a stamp per day, inked once you've finished it. The journey — the four cities drawn to real distances, which is how you explain how far Palermo actually is. Looking back — the trip in numbers, filling in as we go.
Sharing & going homeShare today copies the day's plan as plain text for the family chat, and every stop has its own share button. Navigate to tonight's base walks you home from wherever you are — the one to reach for at the end of a long evening.
08
Tips

Small things worth knowing

Read tomorrow's day brief tonight. It tells you what to wear and what's shut — exactly the argument nobody wants at 7am.
Orange writing means pay attention. It marks anything unbooked, anything closing soon, and anywhere our plan and the actual ticket disagree.
Finishing a list prints a woodcut. Tick off everything in a packing group or a trail and the app stamps it with an inked plate — an empty plate, a folded map, a closed suitcase. Purely a reward.
The 112 button has to be held down. On the emergency screen, press and hold for a second until the bar fills, then let go — that's deliberate, so it can't dial the emergency services from inside a pocket.
The circle at the top right changes the brightness. Tap between dark, light and automatic. It beats evening mode if you'd rather it stayed light.
The card at the top opens up. Tap the countdown for the whole trip on one screen — all four cities, every fixed time we can't move, and a tappable line for each day.
Reset the day if you tick things by mistake. A small Reset button sits next to the progress bar. It only clears that one day.
Times are the tickets, not our wishes. Where the plan said one thing and the ticket said another, the app shows the ticket and explains the difference on the card.
09
If it looks wrong

Don't worry about it

Weather says "avg"The real forecast only exists about two weeks ahead. Until then it shows the usual September temperatures.
A stop has no distance lineThe app only measures distances between places it knows the real coordinates of. Where it isn't certain it says nothing rather than guessing.
No Guide me buttonOn purpose. Things like "clear immigration" or "walk back" aren't places, so there's nothing to point at.
Something says "my suggestion"It's an idea, not a booking. Mostly in Palermo, where we deliberately left the evening loose.
You tapped something oddClose the panel with the ✕, or close and reopen the app. Nothing you do can lose the plan.
One last thing

The plan is a suggestion, not a rulebook. If everyone's tired at four in the afternoon, drop a stop and sit in a piazza instead — every day has something in it that can go without hurting the rest. The app will still be there tomorrow.
