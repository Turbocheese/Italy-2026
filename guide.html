A guide for the six of us

How the itinerary
app works

Everything about our trip lives in one app — where we're going, when, what's booked and what still isn't. Nothing you tap can break it. Have a poke around.

Getting it onto your phone

1
Open the link I sent in the group chat.
2
Add it to your home screen. On an iPhone, tap the share button — the square with the arrow pointing up — and choose Add to Home Screen. On Android, tap the three dots and choose Install.
3
That's it. It now opens like any other app, and it works without internet — which matters, because roaming is expensive and the Vatican has no signal.
02
The main screen

One day at a time

The row of dates at the top

Tap any date to jump to that day. Slide the row sideways for the rest of the trip. Above it are the four cities — tap Rome and you land on our first day there.

The cards down the page

Each stop shows its time, what it is, and everything we know about it — opening hours, why we put it there, how long the walk to the next one takes. Two buttons on each: Maps to see where it is, Walk route for directions on foot.

Ticking things off

Swipe a card to the right and it's done — it fades and gets a line through it. Swipe again to undo. There's a Mark done button inside each card if swiping is fiddly.

Your ticks stay on your own phone and don't show up on anyone else's. Nobody can mess up your copy, and you can't mess up theirs.

At a glance

The bits near the top

Three tilesThe weather, what time the sun sets, and how many hours we're out. If the hours show in red, it's a twelve-hour day — wear the good shoes.
Day briefTap it open. What to wear, what's closed that day of the week, what we do if it rains, and a note about stairs and rest stops for Grandma. Worth reading at breakfast.
The shape of the dayThe little striped bar. Each colour is a kind of stop — sights, tables, markets, rest, moving about. One glance tells you whether the day is packed or gentle. Tap a stripe to jump there.
The quoteA fact about where we are, a heads-up about something tricky, or a note for the family. One per day.

Behind More

The useful drawers

Bookings & numbersEvery flight, train, seat number, booking reference and apartment address, plus emergency numbers. If you only learn one screen, learn this one.
Who carries whatEach of us, our references and our baggage. Important: flying back from Palermo, only Sarah has a suitcase — everyone else is hand luggage. Buy things in Rome, not Sicily.
If you're running earlySixteen good things we cut to make the plan fit, kept per city with hours and map links. For when a day finishes ahead of itself.
Decide before you flyThe handful of things still unbooked, sorted by when they're needed. Tick them as they're sorted.
Italian, just enoughThe phrases we'll actually use, spelled out so you can say them. A table for six, asking for the bill, telling a waiter Ceci can't eat squid, finding a pharmacy.
Packing & baggageA tickable list. Do this before you fly, not the night before.
TrailsThe running themes — every gelato and tiramisu stop, the photo booths, the churches, the wine windows. Tick them off like a collection.
The journeyA map of the four cities drawn to real distances. It's how you explain to people how far Palermo actually is.
PassportA stamp for each day, which inks itself once you've ticked everything on that day. Purely for fun.
Looking backThe trip in numbers — stops done, kilometres covered, tables sat at, gelato count. It fills in as we go.
Arrival modeA big, simple screen with just where we are and where we're going next. Use it while walking.
Share todayCopies the day's plan as plain text, ready to paste into the family chat.
06
Tips

Small things worth knowing

Read tomorrow's day brief tonight. It tells you what to wear and what's shut — exactly the argument nobody wants at 7am.
Orange writing means pay attention. It marks anything unbooked, anything closing soon, and anywhere our plan and the actual ticket disagree.
The circle at the top right changes the brightness. Tap between dark, light and automatic. Dark is easier at dinner; light is easier in the sun.
The card at the top opens up. Tap the countdown for the whole trip on one screen — all four cities, every fixed time we can't move, and a tappable line for each day.
Reset the day if you tick things by mistake. A small Reset button sits next to the progress bar. It only clears that one day.
Times are the tickets, not our wishes. Where the plan said one thing and the ticket said another, the app shows the ticket and explains the difference on the card.
07
If it looks wrong

Don't worry about it

Weather says "avg"The real forecast only exists about two weeks ahead. Until then it shows the usual September temperatures.
A stop has no Maps buttonOn purpose. Things like "clear immigration" or "walk back" aren't places, so there's nothing to point at.
Something says "my suggestion"It's an idea, not a booking. Mostly in Palermo, where we deliberately left the evening loose.
You tapped something oddClose the panel with the ✕, or close and reopen the app. Nothing you do can lose the plan.
One last thing

The plan is a suggestion, not a rulebook. If everyone's tired at four in the afternoon, drop a stop and sit in a piazza instead — every day has something in it that can go without hurting the rest. The app will still be there tomorrow.
