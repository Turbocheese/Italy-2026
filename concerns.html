TOUR D'ITALIA · 17 SEPT – 1 OCT 2026 CONCERNS
Raised 12 August 2026 · from Ryan

Things I think
will break

I walked every one of the 217 stops against its opening hours, its booking, and the time available to reach it. Most of the plan holds up. These are the places it doesn't — ordered by how much damage each one does, not by date.

Blocking · needs a decision before we fly
1 · Six people's luggage cannot come to Palermo

We check out of Via Rattazzi on the 26th and don't check into Via Agostino Valiero until the night of the 27th, so on paper every bag comes with us to Sicily. It can't. Aeroitalia XZ2711 out is a 10 kg cabin bag each with no hold allowance, and ITA AZ1794 back is Economy Light — 8 kg cabin, no hold — for five of the six. Only Sarah has 23 kg checked, on the return only. We have been carrying 30 kg each since Singapore.

What I think we do: leave the large cases in Rome for the two nights. Termini's left luggage runs about €6 per bag for the first five hours then hourly, so five cases for two days is real money but far less than buying hold baggage on two flights. The cleaner option is asking Geneve (Rattazzi) or Lorenzo and Maria Pia (Valiero) to hold them — worth asking now rather than on the day. Either way we go to Palermo with one small bag each, and whatever gets bought in Venice and Florence has to already be in the cases, not in our hands.

Blocking · the timings don't work
2 · CasaMora at 21:30 on the 27th is not reachable

The chain is: land Fiumicino 20:20 → transfer 45–50 min → Valiero 21:15 → dinner 21:30. That leaves nothing at all for getting off the aircraft, walking Terminal 1, and waiting for Sarah's hold bag, which is 20–30 minutes on its own. Realistically we are at the flat between 21:45 and 22:00, and at a table closer to 22:15.

This matters more than a normal late dinner because it is Ceci's actual birthday, and because CasaMora still isn't booked — the note said book it by 16 August, which has passed. Either book it for 22:00 and accept a very late night after a travel day, or move the celebration to the 29th and make the 27th a simple supper near the flat.

Serious, but fixable on the day

3 · St Peter's closes at 19:10, and we arrive at 19:00

The Basilica keeps 07:00–19:10 through September. Our Vatican Museums slot runs 16:00–19:00, so on the current plan we reach the door with ten minutes left — and that assumes we can use the Sistine Chapel's right-hand exit straight into the Basilica, which is at the guards' discretion and often closed to general visitors. If it is shut we walk fifteen minutes round the colonnade and arrive after closing. Either leave the Museums at 18:15 and accept losing the last stretch, or move the Basilica to the morning of the 26th — except we fly to Palermo that day, so realistically it's the former.

4 · Poesis Firenze at 16:45 has no room in it

My own addition, and it doesn't fit as scheduled. Boboli and Pitti run 14:45–16:45, San Miniato is 17:00, and the walk up is twenty minutes — so Poesis gets minus five minutes. It's my friend's shop and I'd rather do it properly than wave through the door. Suggest trimming Boboli to 90 minutes, giving Poesis 16:15–16:45, and pushing San Miniato to 17:15 — sunset isn't until 19:18, so there's slack at the end of that day, not the middle.

5 · Six of us will not fit in one gondola

Gondolas take five passengers. The 15:30 ride on the 19th is booked as one line item and one thirty-minute slot — it needs to be two gondolas, and the price doubles. Worth agreeing the split before we're standing at the jetty, and worth knowing the daytime rate is roughly €90 per gondola, so about €180 rather than €90.

6 · The Istanbul layover is 1h35m, and everything downstream depends on it

Legal, but Istanbul is an enormous airport and 1h35 assumes an on-time arrival at 05:15 and a gate that isn't at the far end. If we miss it, the next Venice service costs us most of Day 1 — the Accademia is booked for 11:26 and closes Mondays, so it can't simply move to the 21st. Nothing to fix, just worth everyone knowing that the connection is the single point of failure for the whole first day, and worth having Turkish's app installed with the PNR in it before we go.

7 · Day 1 asks a lot after fourteen hours of flying

We land at 08:20 having left Singapore at 23:05, then walk Dorsoduro from 11:00 until dinner at 19:30 — around eight hours on our feet, over Venice's stepped bridges, before anyone has slept in a bed. The 16:08 buffer helps. I'd flag the Accademia at 11:26 as the one fixed point and treat everything between it and the 17:30 check-in as genuinely optional, especially for Grandma. Better to drop two shops than to start the trip exhausted.

Smaller things, listed so they aren't forgotten

Badia Fiorentina
21 Sept		Open Mondays only, 15:00–18:00. We check in at 15:00 and are due there at 15:45 — with the bags still to collect from storage 370 m away. It works, but there is no slack in it.
Da Gino
25 Sept		Takes no online bookings, serves 12:45–14:45, and we arrive at 13:18 needing to leave by 14:38 for the Vatican. A thirty-minute wait for six would put the fixed 16:00 entry at risk. Call from the Pantheon queue that morning.
Galleria Colonna
25 Sept		Fridays and Saturdays, 09:30–13:00 — the only window in our whole fortnight. If the Pantheon runs over, cut Piazza Navona rather than this.
Prosciutteria
29 Sept		Walk-in only, no reservations at all, and it's Mum and Dad's anniversary dinner. Arrive at 19:30 rather than 20:00 sharp, or send two people ahead to hold a table.
Ferramenta
26 Sept		Grilled squid is one of its signature dishes, and it's our late-night suggestion in Palermo. Worth saying out loud for Ceci before we order anything to share.
Sunday in Palermo
27 Sept		The 12:30 free block is built around shopping on Via Roma and Via Maqueda, and Sicilian shops keep short or closed Sundays. The markets and street food stay open; the shops mostly won't. Centro Ricambi Moto in particular is worth a call before Dad walks over.
The 28th's crawl
28 Sept		Thirteen vintage shops in ninety minutes is roughly seven minutes each including the walking between them. Nobody is finishing that list — better to pick four properly than to jog past all thirteen.
